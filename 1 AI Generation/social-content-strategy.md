# Social content strategy — the two Fable avatars

Drafted 2026-09-29 from the user's answers. Not n8n yet: the user said the AI still has to be trained first, so everything here is **manual** until the content proves itself. Automate later, once we know what works.

## Decisions (stated by the user)
- **Two AI avatars**, both South Asian, both speaking English: an attractive **female** presenter and a very **gentlemanly male** presenter.
- **Job:** fashion sense and fashion advice, not product ads. The avatars build an audience first.
- **Platforms, all four:** Facebook Reels + Page, Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts. One master vertical video, cross-posted.
- **Cadence:** 3–4 posts per week at launch.
- **Content types to test first:** styling advice, outfit of the day, fashion tips and do's and don'ts.
- **Products on screen:** mostly none at first. Styling only; Fable pieces come in later.
- **Captions:** English or Bangla, **decided per post** (log the choice, see the tracker below).
- **Tool budget:** free first, up to **USD 100/month** later.
- **Market:** Bangladesh, ages 15–30.
- **Brand rule from CONTEXT:** the phrase "old money" is never used anywhere.

## Content pillars (3, rotate)
1. **Styling advice** — "How to wear one shirt three ways", "what to wear for X occasion". The core.
2. **Outfit of the day** — avatar in a styled look, short voiceover on why it works. No Fable branding yet.
3. **Do's and don'ts** — "5 fit mistakes", "colours that work together". Cheap to make and easy to share.

**Weekly rhythm (3–4 posts):** Styling advice x2, OOTD x1, Do's and don'ts x1 (skip the fourth in a light week). Alternate which avatar fronts each video so both build recognition.

## Posting times (Asia/Dhaka)
Best window is roughly **8:00–11:00 PM**. Facebook peaks about 8–10:30 PM, Instagram about 9–11 PM, TikTok 9 PM to past midnight. Weekday backup: 1:00–2:30 PM. Start with 9 PM for everything, then compare per platform after two weeks.

## Producing the avatars
Two separate jobs:
1. **A consistent face and look** for each avatar (same person every video).
2. **Talking video** (lip-sync, voice).

| Tool | Good for | Cost (verify, sources disagree) |
|---|---|---|
| Higgsfield (Soul ID) | Consistent character images, plus video and lip-sync | Free tier has no video and no commercial use; paid ~USD 15–49/mo, 100+ credits per few clips |
| HeyGen | Most realistic talking head, 175+ languages | Free = 3 watermarked videos; paid ~USD 12–29/mo |
| Hedra | Photo + voice to talking video, cheapest per minute | Free 100 credits, watermarked; ~USD 15–30/mo |

**Suggested path:** Phase 0 (free) test each tool on one avatar and compare face consistency and lip-sync. Watermarked output cannot be posted. Phase 1 (about USD 30–50/mo) subscribe to one tool for images plus one for talking video, whichever tested better. Stay under the USD 100 cap. Decide the winner from your own tests, not from these reviews.

## Voices
Options, in order of effort:
1. **Built-in voices** in HeyGen / Hedra / Higgsfield. Simplest, and lip-sync is automatic. Limited choice of South Asian English accents.
2. **ElevenLabs** (separate voice tool): Voice Design creates an original voice from a text description (age, tone, pacing, accent), plus a voice library with many South Asian English accents. Free ~10k credits/month (~10 min), but the free plan has **no commercial license and requires attribution**. Starter (~USD 6/mo, 30k credits) adds commercial rights. Verify on their pricing page.

**Workflow:** design one voice per avatar in ElevenLabs, save its voice ID, and upload the audio into the avatar tool (Hedra and HeyGen accept your own audio). Use that same voice on every video so each avatar sounds like one person.

## Free long-term voices (researched 2026-09-29, verify licences before commercial use)
Free forever means open-source models run on your own machine, not a subscription. Hermes Agent is only a wrapper that calls TTS providers (its local options are small ones like Piper), it does not create better voices itself.

| Model | What it gives you | Licence (commercial?) |
|---|---|---|
| **Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign** (Alibaba) | Creates a brand-new voice from a text description, the closest free match to ElevenLabs Voice Design. Runs on ~4 GB VRAM | Apache 2.0 (yes) |
| **VoxCPM2** (OpenBMB) | Voice design from text, plus cloning from a short clip. 2B params, released Apr 2026 | Apache 2.0 (yes) |
| **Chatterbox** (Resemble AI) | Clones a voice from ~10s of audio, reported to beat ElevenLabs in blind tests | MIT (yes) |
| Kokoro | Tiny and fast, but no cloning and fixed voices | Apache 2.0 (yes) |
| F5-TTS | Good quality | CC-BY-NC, **not commercial**, avoid |
| Fish Speech | Strong cloning | Open weights need a **paid licence for commercial use**, avoid |

**Recommended path:** design each avatar's voice with Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign (use the two prompts above), save the best clip, then use that clip as the reference for Chatterbox or VoxCPM2 so the voice stays the same across every video (inference on my part, test that it holds). Needs a GPU; if the laptop has none, run it free on Hugging Face Spaces or Google Colab. Whether the accent lands "polished with a faint South Asian hint" is untested, judge by ear.

**Fastest start:** ElevenLabs free tier for testing now, open-source for the long-term free route once the voice brief is settled.

## Realism goal
**Ultra realism** is the target (user decision, 2026-09-29): skin texture, micro-expressions, natural pacing, real-looking settings, tight lip-sync. The avatars are **always labeled as AI** (platform toggle + bio line), which the user confirmed. Judge tools on realism first.

## Guardrails
- **Label the avatars as AI** in the bio and per Meta and TikTok's AI-content labels. It protects the account and the brand's trust.
- **No real person's face or likeness.** Generate original characters only.
- **Local tone:** advice should be styled for Bangladeshi weather, occasions and dress norms. Keep the female avatar's presentation tasteful for the audience.
- **Do not show products you cannot ship.** Which is why launch content stays styling-only.

## What each video needs
Hook in the first 2 seconds, one clear tip, on-screen text, end on one call to action ("follow for more", later "link in bio"). Vertical 9:16, under 30 seconds to start.
